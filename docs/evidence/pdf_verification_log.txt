Stimulus Control and Sleep Hygiene Protocol for 
Sleep-Onset Insomnia
Introduction
This evidence-based Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Insomnia (CBT-I) protocol is designed for adults experiencing 
difficulty initiating sleep. Stimulus control strengthens the conditioned association between the bed and rapid sleep 
onset, while sleep hygiene optimizes physiological readiness for rest.
Goals
-
Re-establish the bed and bedroom as strong discriminative stimuli for sleep rather than frustration or alertness.
-
Maintain a consistent sleep-wake schedule to consolidate homeostatic sleep drive.
-
Eliminate sleep-incompatible autonomic arousal in the bedroom.
Step-by-Step Instructions
Step 1: The 20-Minute Re-Association Rule
Go to bed only when genuinely sleepy. If you have not fallen asleep within approximately 20 minutes:
-
Leave the bedroom immediately.
-
Move to a dimly lit room and engage in a quiet, non-screen activity (e.g., reading a physical book, gentle somatic 
relaxation).
-
Return to bed only when drowsiness returns. Repeat as often as necessary throughout the night.
Step 2: Bedroom Exclusivity
Do not use the bed for any activities other than sleep and sexual intimacy. Reading, watching television, checking 
phones, eating, or working must occur outside the bedroom.
Step 3: Anchor Wake Time
Set a non-negotiable wake-up time 7 days a week, regardless of how much sleep you obtained the previous night. 
Do not compensate with daytime naps.

Practice and Homework
-
Keep a 7-day sleep diary recording bed time, estimated sleep onset latency, night awakenings, and final rising 
time.
-
Implement the 20-minute rule consistently every night for the upcoming 14 days without exception.
Reflection and Progress Review
-
Did you notice any anticipatory anxiety when getting into bed?
-
How many times did you leave the bedroom before sleep onset occurred?
-
What calming activity proved most effective during the out-of-bed interval?
Conclusion
Conditioned insomnia develops gradually and requires 2-4 weeks of consistent behavioral practice to extinguish. 
If insomnia symptoms persist beyond 4 weeks or if daytime impairment impairs safety, consult a licensed clinical 
psychologist or sleep medicine physician for personalized care.
